# Runtime shoulder measurements and coupled capacity

This change measures the achieved shoulder state after composition and support IK. Reports, captured frames and kinematic exports carry rest-relative arm elevation, plane and rest-axis twist in the live thorax and girdle frames. Missing references and singular planes remain explicit. These are measurements of the existing single-girdle proxy, not independent GH/SC/AC/ST measurements.

`shoulderCapacity: 'enforce-proxy'` selects a coupled rest-relative arm cone for a composed motion. The default engineering budget is 120 degrees, replaceable through `constraints.R_UpperArm.girdleProxyElevation.availableRange.max` (and L). This reserved field is an engineering control, not patient clinical ROM. The new upper-reach, push-up and bird-dog recipes opt in; unmarked older recipes retain their previous behavior. Existing patient ROM bounds take priority if constraints have no joint solution; the remaining capacity violation is reported and fails the capacity check.

The shared projector preserves arm axial twist and explicit girdle intent, runs after hand IK, and is used by both the stage and sampler. A displaced hand reports its geometric support residual rather than implying support force. Constraint changes invalidate cached hand-reach solutions. Single commands and composed shoulder keyframes report realized shortfalls or unavailable projected measurements separately from completion of playback. Captured frames retain their actual transforms on replay; editing clears stale measurements, and exports include shoulder metadata.

Validation covers all three production rigs, both sides, axial rotations, combined elevations, root/trunk frame changes, patient limits, unreachable hand targets, 60 Hz sampling, export/replay and missing measurement rejection. The existing hand-plant/latch regressions pass at 30/60/120 Hz without relaxed tolerances. A live 27-case rendered review completed without browser errors, with constrained-overhead elevation at 119.999998 degrees for each rig; a subsequent three-rig review checks final outcome reporting. These engineering checks do not constitute anatomical calibration or clinical acceptance.
